Your relationship to the model

To you the model is a risk to be understood and managed: to people, to the organization, and to compliance. You don't usually build or run it. You set the rules it operates under, test it adversarially, and hold the evidence that it behaves as claimed.

This org covers AI governance — governing, securing and assuring AI: inventories and risk assessments, red-teaming and adversarial ML, release assurance and evaluation evidence, ML security, agentic safety, enterprise control architecture, and executive leadership of an AI governance program.

What you own

  • Policy: acceptable use, risk tiers, and approval gates
  • Risk and impact assessments mapped to frameworks (NIST AI RMF, ISO/IEC 42001, EU AI Act)
  • Independent evaluation, red-teaming, and safety testing
  • Security of AI systems: prompt injection, data leakage, and supply chain
  • Model inventory, documentation, audit, and incident review

What you don't own: The implementation. You define what 'safe enough' means and verify it; the builders and operators make it so.

Who you work with

No curriculum owns the model end to end. These are the handoffs with each of the others.

  • AI Workforce

    Work alongside the models

    You hand them
    Clear rules on what may be shared with which tools, and which uses need review, written so people can follow them
    They hand you
    How AI is actually being used on the ground, including the shadow uses policy hasn't caught up with

  • AI Startup

    Turn it into a company

    You hand them
    A defensible position for customers, regulators, and investors, and early warning on uses that would sink the company
    They hand you
    The company's risk appetite, target markets, and customer commitments that set the compliance bar

  • AI Engineering

    Build with the models

    You hand them
    Risk tiers, guardrail requirements, and red-team findings to fix before (and after) launch
    They hand you
    System descriptions, eval results, and guardrail designs for each AI feature, plus incident reports when something goes wrong

  • ML Engineering

    Build and train the models

    You hand them
    Data-use rules, documentation standards, and the risk-based evaluation bar a model must clear
    They hand you
    Data provenance, model cards, training-data documentation, and evaluation results they can audit

  • AI Infrastructure

    Run the platforms models live on

    You hand them
    Control requirements (logging, access, retention, residency) and the evidence they need to produce
    They hand you
    Audit logs, access controls, data-residency guarantees, and evidence that controls actually run in production

How the tracks work

Learn

Each track is a sequence of modules: lecture chapters, hands-on exercises, quizzes, and projects. Start with Module 00 and work forward.

Check your work

The paired solutions repo has a reference walkthrough for every exercise. Attempt each one first; the gap between your answer and the reference is where the learning happens.

Built in the open

The content is written with AI assistance and under ongoing human review, so it can contain mistakes. Report anything wrong through the repo's Issues; fixes ship continuously.
